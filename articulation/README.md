# The Articulation Track

Building something good is half the job. Explaining it clearly is the other half. The Articulation Track runs alongside your technical work for all 12 weeks, as part of the Interview Track's Communication area.

## How it works

- **Every session** has a short speaking drill. Pairs first when the group is large. While we're a small group, we go round the room in an order you'll know in advance, so nobody gets a surprise first turn.
- **Every week** has one homework task: something you record or write, and then review yourself.
- **Same rubric every week**, so you can see your own line move. In Week 12 you re-record your Week 1 introduction and compare.

Think in Tamil if it helps. Rehearse in any language. Deliver in English. Content first, polish second.

## Where your homework goes

One file per week in this folder: `week01-introduction.md`, then `week02-...md` and so on. Your mentor tells you the topic each week. Commit the file in your pull request.

**Recordings are not committed to this public repo.** Record on your phone or laptop, then send it to Ravi on WhatsApp, or upload it to your Google Drive, keep General access **Restricted**, share it with **fellowship@hybr.com** as a viewer and paste the link in your file. Do not use "anyone with the link" and do not post it on social media. You can delete the recording whenever you like.

## Giving and receiving feedback

Use **I like, I wish, What if**: one thing that worked, one thing you would change, one idea to try. Comment on what was said, not on accent or grammar.

## Speaking habits

Do:
- Say the answer in your first sentence, then the reason.
- Give one real example: a project, a bug, a person.
- Use short sentences. One idea, then stop.
- Pause instead of saying "um".
- Say the number and what it means.
- Finish with a next step or an ask.

Do not:
- Start with a long background story.
- Read from a script word for word.
- Apologise before you begin.
- Hide in jargon when a simple word will do.
- Compare your Week 1 with someone else's Week 12. Compare yourself with yourself.

## The rubric

Five criteria, four levels. Scores are for learning, not ranking. Nobody is graded or excluded by them. Peers score one criterion each week; mentors score all five on a sample. Write your own scores in your weekly file so you can see your trend.

| Criterion | 1 Starting | 2 Developing | 3 Solid | 4 Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Answer first and structure** | No clear main point | Main point appears late or is unclear | Main point in the first two sentences; some order | Main point first; grouped reasons; the listener can follow without effort |
| **B. Concrete evidence or example** | Only general statements | An example is hinted at, but vague | One specific example with a detail | Specific example with a detail and a result or number you can source |
| **C. Clarity of language** | Jargon or long sentences make it hard to follow | Understandable with effort | Mostly plain, short sentences; terms explained | Plain and precise; an outsider could follow |
| **D. Delivery: pace, pauses, fillers** | Rushed or halting; fillers distract | Some pauses; fillers frequent | Steady pace; fillers occasional | Deliberate pauses; fillers rare; sounds like a conversation |
| **E. Close and next step** | Stops abruptly | Ends with a generic line | States a next step or ask | States a specific next step or ask the listener can act on |
